Get started with SBC Money

Envelope budgeting on top of your own Tiller spreadsheet. You get your own copy; your bank data stays in spreadsheets you own. This page walks you through it, in order.

1

A Google account

Before anything

Everything here lives in Google Sheets, so you sign in to SBC Money with a Google account. It does not have to be a Gmail address.

Do you already have a Google account?

Good. Use that account for every step below — the one you'll sign in to SBC Money with. If you have several, pick one now and stick with it.

Make one on the email you already have (Outlook, iCloud, work — anything). Two minutes, no new mailbox:

  1. Open accounts.google.com/signup → For myself.
  2. On the email step choose Use my current email address instead and type your usual address.
  3. Enter the code Google emails you. Done — that address is now a Google account.
2

Tiller — your bank feed

Day 1 · then wait a night

Tiller pulls your bank, card and loan transactions into a Google Sheet every day. SBC Money reads that sheet. Do you have Tiller set up already?

  1. Go to tiller.com and start a subscription signed in as the Google account from step 1.
  2. Pick Google Sheets (not Excel) and the Tiller Foundation Template.
  3. In the Tiller Console, link the bank, credit-card and loan accounts you want to budget. The sheet it makes is your Tiller sheet, in your own Google Drive.
  4. Leave it overnight. Tiller's first full fill (transactions + balance history) lands on the next morning's run. Come back to step 3 tomorrow.
Then you're a day ahead. One thing to check before you go on:

Tidy the Categories tab in your Tiller sheet — it becomes your envelopes, name for name (emoji included):

  • One row per envelope you want. Give every row a Group and a Type (Expense / Income / Transfer).
  • Tick Hide on anything you won't budget. Hidden rows are skipped.
  • Renames later go through the app, not through Tiller — so get the names how you like them now.

Keep the Tiller sheet's address handy (the browser URL while it's open). The setup wizard asks for it.

3

Make your own copy of the SBC sheet

Day 2 · 1 minute

SBC Money keeps your envelopes in a second sheet next to Tiller's — the companion sheet. You copy a blank template; the copy is yours, and it carries the setup wizard with it.

First, make sure you're signed in to Google as the account from step 1. If you have more than one Google account, the copy lands in whichever one is active — and it has to be the one you'll sign in to SBC Money with.

Make my copy

The template link isn't set on this page yet. Ask for it — you'll get a link that ends in /copy.
  1. Google asks Copy document? — name it SBC Money — your name (Companion) and press Make a copy. It opens in a new tab.
  2. Keep that tab open for step 4. Don't type anything into the sheet; the wizard builds every tab itself.
4

Run the setup wizard

Day 2 · 5 minutes

In your copy, open the menu Envelope Budget → Set up SBC Money (new user wizard). (The menu appears a few seconds after the sheet loads. If it isn't there, reload the page.)

The first click shows Google's authorization screens. This is normal: it's your own copy of the script asking for permission to work on your own sheets. Nobody else is granted anything. The screens look like this — go through them exactly:

Authorization required → press Continue → choose your account.
Google hasn't verified this app → press Advanced (small, bottom-left) → Go to SBC Money (unsafe).
SBC Money wants access to your Google Account → press Allow.

Then open the menu item once more — the wizard opens. Six short steps:

  1. Welcome — checks you're in your copy, signed in as its owner. Add your first name if you like.
  2. Tiller — paste your Tiller sheet's address and press Check. Green means ready; amber tells you what to tidy in Tiller but lets you carry on.
  3. Envelopes — your Tiller categories, as envelopes. Untick any you don't want; keep the optional Savings Buffer; set your time zone.
  4. Create — the only step that writes. Builds your companion sheet. You're the only admin.
  5. Share — one button: your sheets are shared with the app's own account (editor — it imports hourly and writes your categorisations back) and a read-only account that feeds the fast view. Not with any person.
  6. Finish — the sheet registers itself with SBC Money and runs your first import. About 20 seconds. If it says You're in, you are.
If Finish says "Not registered yet"

It tells you why — nearly always a share from step 5 that didn't take. Open that sheet → Share → paste the address it names → pick the role → untick Notify people → Share, then press Try again. If it keeps failing, use the Email the code button on that screen; a person registers you by hand the same day.

If the wizard won't open at all

Use Envelope Budget → Migration → Setup — classic prompts (fallback). It asks the same questions one at a time, then do the four shares by hand and email the code it gives you.

5

Sign in and put it on your phone

2 minutes
  1. On your phone, open sbc.theaubuchonfamily.com → Sign in with Google → the account from step 1.
  2. Share → Add to Home Screen (iPhone: the share square, scroll down; Android: the ⋮ menu → Add to Home screen). It then opens like an app, full screen.
  3. First session, in this order: Inbox → approve the first batch · Balances → Reconcile to bank into Income Cash Pool · Fund from pool. The CC Payment 💳 envelope is pass-through — never fund it.

Who can see your money

Plainly
Your bank data lives in your Tiller spreadsheet and your companion spreadsheet, in your Google Drive, which you own. Two service identities get access so the app works: an app account (editor — it imports your transactions hourly and writes your categorisations back) and a read-only account that feeds the fast view. No person is shared on either sheet, nobody else is on your app's access list, and nothing in the app shows anyone your numbers. One honest caveat: someone runs the server the app uses (a database and the app account's password), so like any app's operator they could technically query it. The app keeps a visible record of every such read — Settings → Access → Who has read the database — so you'd see it. You can leave any time: Tiller keeps your complete history in your own spreadsheet.
