Get started with SBC Money

A Google account

Before anything

Everything here lives in Google Sheets, so you sign in to SBC Money with a Google account. It does not have to be a Gmail address.

Tip: if you already use Tiller, use the Google account your Tiller spreadsheet lives in. Whatever you pick here is the account for every step below.

Do you already have a Google account?

Good. If you have several, pick one now and stick with it.

Two minutes at accounts.google.com/signup → For myself. On the email step you can either make a new Gmail address or choose Use my current email address instead and keep the email you already have (Outlook, iCloud, work — anything). Either way, that's the account you'll use for everything below.

2

Tiller — your bank feed

Day 1 · then wait a night

Tiller pulls your bank, credit card and loan transactions into a Google Sheet every day. SBC Money reads that sheet. Do you have Tiller set up already?

  1. Go to tiller.com and start a subscription signed in as the Google account from step 1.
  2. Pick Google Sheets (not Excel) and the Tiller Foundation Template.
  3. In the Tiller Console, link the bank, credit card and loan accounts you want to budget. The sheet it makes is your Tiller sheet, in your own Google Drive.
  4. Leave it overnight. Tiller's first full fill (transactions + balance history) lands on the next morning's run. Come back to step 3 tomorrow.
Then you're a day ahead. One thing to check before you go on:

Tidy the Categories tab in your Tiller sheet — it becomes your envelopes, name for name (emoji included):

  • One row per envelope you want. Give every row a Group and a Type (Expense / Income / Transfer).
  • Tick Hide on anything you won't budget. Hidden rows are skipped.
  • Renames later go through the app, not through Tiller — so get the names how you like them now.

Keep the Tiller sheet's address handy (the browser URL while it's open). The setup wizard asks for it.

3

Make your own copy of the SBC sheet

Day 2 · 1 minute

SBC Money keeps your envelopes in a second sheet next to Tiller's — the companion sheet. You copy a blank template; the copy is yours, and it carries the setup wizard with it.

First, make sure you're signed in to Google as the account from step 1. If you have more than one Google account, the copy lands in whichever one is active — and it has to be the one you'll sign in to SBC Money with.

Make my copy

The template link isn't set on this page yet. Ask for it — you'll get a link that ends in /copy.
  1. Google asks Copy document? and notes that the attached Apps Script file is copied too — that's the setup wizard. Press Make a copy. It opens in a new tab, named Copy of SBC Money — Companion…; the wizard renames it to SBC Money - your name (Companion) in step 4.
  2. Keep that tab open for step 4. Don't type anything into the sheet; the wizard builds every tab itself.
4

Run the setup wizard

Day 2 · 5 minutes

In your copy of the (Companion) sheet — the tab that opened in step 3 — open the menu Envelope Budget → Set up SBC Money (new user wizard). (The menu appears a few seconds after the sheet loads. If it isn't there, reload the page.)

The first click shows Google's permission screens. This is normal, and it is the one part that looks alarming: your copy of the sheet carries its own copy of the setup script, and Google has never seen that copy before, so it calls it unverified. The "developer" the screens mention is you — the account that owns the copy. Nobody else is granted anything. The screens look like this; the red marks show the five clicks:

Authorization required → press Continue → choose your account (the one from step 1).
Google hasn't verified this app → press Advanced (small, bottom-left) → Go to SBC Money (unsafe).
Sign in to SBC Money → press Continue.
SBC Money wants access to your Google Account → tick Select all → scroll down → press Continue.

The address shown as the developer on these screens is your own; the pictures use you@gmail.com in its place. If you pressed Back to safety or Cancel by mistake, just open the menu item again.

Then open the menu item once more — the wizard opens. Six short steps:

  1. Welcome — checks you're in your copy, signed in as its owner. Add your first name if you like.
  2. Tiller — paste your Tiller sheet's address and press Check. Green means ready; amber tells you what to tidy in Tiller but lets you carry on.
  3. Envelopes — your Tiller categories, as envelopes. Untick any you don't want and set your time zone. The optional Savings Buffer is one extra envelope for getting a month ahead: money you set aside now so next month's bills are already covered before the month starts. Keep it unless you know you won't use it.
  4. Create — the only step that writes. Builds your companion sheet. You're the only admin.
  5. Share — one button: your sheets are shared with the app's own account (editor — it imports hourly and writes your categorisations back) and a read-only account that feeds the fast view. Not with any person.
  6. Finish — the sheet registers itself with SBC Money and runs your first import. About 20 seconds. If it says You're in, you are.
If Finish says "Not registered yet"

It tells you why — nearly always a share from step 5 that didn't take. Open that sheet → Share → paste the address it names → pick the role → untick Notify people → Share, then press Try again. If it keeps failing, use the Email the code button on that screen and you'll be registered by hand.

If the wizard won't open at all

Use Envelope Budget → Migration → Setup — classic prompts (fallback). It asks the same questions one at a time, then do the four shares by hand and email the code it gives you.

Who can see your money

Plainly
Your bank data lives in your Tiller spreadsheet and your companion spreadsheet, in your Google Drive, which you own. Two service identities get access so the app works: an app account (editor — it imports your transactions hourly and writes your categorisations back) and a read-only account that feeds the fast view. No person is shared on either sheet, nobody else is on your app's access list, and nothing in the app shows anyone your numbers. Your bank login never leaves Tiller, and the sheets hold only account names with the last four digits — nobody, operator included, can see full account numbers or passwords. One honest caveat: someone runs the server the app uses (a database) and holds the app account's password, so like any app's operator they could technically look. Reading the database leaves a mark: every statement anyone other than the app itself runs against it is recorded and shown to you under Settings → Account & privacy → Who has read the database, which also says whether that recording is switched on. Opening your sheets in Google Drive as the app account is not in that record — the sheet's own Activity panel in Google Sheets is the record there. You can leave any time — Settings → Account & privacy → Delete my account removes everything of yours from the app, and Tiller keeps your complete history in your own spreadsheet. The full privacy page says the same in a few more words.
