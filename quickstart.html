💡The big idea

SBC Money is envelope budgeting. Picture cash envelopes with names on them: Rent, Groceries, Eating Out. Every dollar you have sits in exactly one envelope — that is the dollar's job. Two rules make the whole system work:

1

Only budget money you actually have. Not next week's paycheck, not what you hope to earn — just what is in your accounts right now.

2

Before you spend, look at the envelope, not the bank account. Your bank balance lies to you: it includes rent money that isn't spent yet. The envelope tells the truth about what is really free to spend.

Do that consistently and something great happens: you stop living paycheck to paycheck, because each envelope fills up before its bill arrives. The long-term goal is to be a full month ahead.

📚Six words you'll see everywhere

Envelope
A named job for money (Groceries, Rent). Its balance is what is truly available for that job. Envelopes live in groups (Bills, Everyday, Savings).
Income Cash Pool
The loading dock. New money lands here first, then you deal it out. Money in the pool has no job yet. Available to fund on Home is the pool, and nothing else.
Fund
Moving money from the pool into envelopes — "giving every dollar a job." A funding plan is a saved recipe ("every paycheck: $500 rent, $200 groceries, the rest to savings") so payday takes one tap.
Inbox / Approve
New bank transactions waiting for you to confirm which envelope they belong to. Approve is that confirmation; the envelope moves the moment you do.
Rollover
Envelope balances carry forward forever. Grocery money you didn't spend this month is still yours next month.
Reconcile
Making the envelopes add up to what the bank actually holds. You do it once on day one; the app checks it quietly after that.

🚀Day one — put your real money on the books (5 minutes, once)

When you first sign in, your envelopes already exist — one per category in your Tiller sheet — and every one of them is $0. That is on purpose: your history is kept, but budgeting starts today. The Inbox only shows transactions from today onward.

  1. Open Balances and check that the bank balances look right.
  2. Settings → Maintenance → Reconcile to bank. SBC puts the cash you actually have into the Income Cash Pool.
  3. Open Fund and deal that money out — this month's rent, groceries until payday, a little in each envelope you'll touch before the next paycheck. Leave what's left in the pool or park it in savings.
Leave 💳 CC Payment at $0. It is plumbing, not a spending envelope — the credit-card section below explains why.

From here on it is the routine below. Give it two paydays before judging it; the first one is always the slowest.

💵Payday — the routine that matters most

Three steps, about two minutes:

  1. Approve the paycheck. Open Inbox — your deposit is waiting. Approve it into your income envelope (Regular Pay 💵).
  2. Move it to the pool. Open the Regular Pay envelope and tap → Move $… to pool. (No "are you sure" — the message after has Undo.)
  3. Fund your envelopes. Tap Available to fund on Home (or More → Fund on a phone). Apply your funding plan, or split it by hand, and confirm when the footer says Fully assigned ✓.

That is it — every dollar of the paycheck has a job. Money left over goes to a savings envelope; never leave dollars jobless in the pool for long. Paid more than once a month? Save a plan per paycheck and give each its How often.

🧾During the week — approve your spending

As you spend, transactions flow into the Inbox on their own (from your bank, every hour). A few times a week, clear it:

  • Most items arrive with a suggestion — tap the envelope button to approve it if it's right. Under ⋯, turn on Always suggest once and that store is suggested automatically from then on.
  • ✓ Approve all takes everything that already has a suggestion.
  • One purchase, two jobs? Split spreads it across envelopes (the Walmart run that was half groceries, half a game).

Approving isn't busywork — it is the moment you see your spending. That ten-second glance is what makes the budget real.

🚨When an envelope runs dry

Eating Out is empty and it's only the 20th? That is the system working — it caught the overspend while you can still do something about it.

  • Cover it: open an envelope with slack and use Move money. You are choosing the trade-off ("less clothes money this month") instead of discovering it later.
  • Or let it ride negative: negative envelopes are allowed — they stay red and roll forward until you cover them at the next funding. Nothing hides.

Moving money between envelopes is normal and healthy. The budget is a plan, not a punishment.

💳Credit cards — and why CC Payment stays empty

Cards are the one thing that trips people up, and the rule is simpler than you'd guess: a card purchase is spending, and it counts the moment you swipe. When the charge arrives in your Inbox you approve it into Groceries or Gas like any other purchase, and that envelope goes down right then.

So don't put money in CC Payment 💳. When you pay the card bill, money leaves checking and the card balance drops; both sides land in CC Payment and cancel out, which is why it sits at about $0. Funding it would count the same spending twice.

On Home, Cash in accounts is what's in checking and savings, and Cards owed (amber) is the part that belongs to the card companies. Amber, not red — an obligation, not a problem.
