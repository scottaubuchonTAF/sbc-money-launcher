Start here

💡 The big idea (read this first)

SBC Money is envelope budgeting. Picture cash envelopes with names on them: Rent, Groceries, Eating Out. Every dollar you have sits in exactly one envelope — that is the dollar’s job.

Two rules make the whole system work:

1. Only budget money you actually have. Not next week’s paycheck, not what you hope to earn — just what is in your accounts right now.

2. Before you spend, look at the envelope, not the bank account. Your bank balance lies to you: it includes rent money that isn’t spent yet. The envelope tells the truth about what is really free to spend.

Do that consistently and something great happens: you stop living paycheck to paycheck, because each envelope fills up before its bill arrives. The long-term goal is to be a full month ahead.

🚀 Your first day

Sign in at sbc.theaubuchonfamily.com with the Google account you gave Scott — any Google account works once it is registered. On a phone, add it to your Home Screen (Share → Add to Home Screen on iPhone; the browser menu → Install app on Android) so it opens like an app.

What you will see. Your envelopes already exist — one per category in your Tiller sheet — and every one of them is $0. That is on purpose: your history is kept, but budgeting starts today. The Inbox only shows transactions from today onward.

Put your real money on the books (5 minutes, once). Open Balances and check that the bank balances look right. Then Settings → Maintenance → Reconcile accounts → Reconcile to bank: SBC puts the cash you actually have into the Income Cash Pool. Now open Fund and deal that money out — this month’s rent, groceries until payday, a little in each envelope you will touch before the next paycheck. Leave what is left in the pool or park it in savings.

Leave 💳 CC Payment at $0. It is plumbing, not a spending envelope — the credit-card section below explains why.

From here on it is the routine in the next section. Give it two paydays before judging it; the first one is always the slowest.

📚 Words you’ll see everywhere

• Envelope — a named job for money (Groceries, Rent). Its balance is what is truly available for that job.

• Group — a folder of envelopes (Bills, Everyday, Savings).

• Income Cash Pool — the loading dock. New money lands here first, then you deal it out. Money in the pool has no job yet.

• Available to fund on Home is the pool, and nothing else — the same number Fund will let you deal out. Income parked in another income envelope (a Regular Pay, say) is listed under it as In Regular Pay: it is your money, it just isn’t fundable until you open that envelope and tap Move to pool.

• Fund — moving money from the pool into envelopes. This is “giving every dollar a job.”

• Funding plan — a saved recipe (“every paycheck: $500 rent, $200 groceries, the rest to savings”) so payday takes one tap.

• Budget — what you mean to fund this month. It comes from your funding plans; Home’s Funded card compares the two.

• Inbox — new bank transactions waiting for you to confirm which envelope they belong to. Approve is that confirmation; the envelope moves the moment you do.

• Rollover — envelope balances carry forward forever. Grocery money you didn’t spend this month is still yours next month.

• Goal — an envelope you’re saving up in, with a number to reach and (if you like) a date. Max — a ceiling for an envelope; it can go over, and SBC suggests moving the extra.

• Obligation — a payment that leaves on its own: a card autopay, a mortgage, a car loan. Envelopes say the money is spoken for; an obligation says which account it leaves from, and when.

• Tag — a label that cuts across envelopes (“Myrtle Beach 2027”) so you can see what an event cost.

• Moved to / Moved from / Funded from — in an envelope’s Activity, each money movement names the other envelope involved; tap the name to jump there.

• Maintenance — a page under Settings holding the tools for when something looks wrong: Tiller sync, Reconcile accounts, Remove a transaction, Access, the sheet links.

• Tiller — the service that pulls your bank data into your own Google Sheet. SBC reads from that sheet and writes your decisions back to it. The sheet is the record; the app is the fast view.

Write-back — every save you make here is also written to your sheet in the background (the “syncing” toast). You never need to open the sheet, but you always can.

The routine

💵 Payday — the routine that matters most

Three steps, about two minutes:

• Approve the paycheck. Open Inbox — your deposit is waiting. Approve it into your income envelope (Regular Pay 💵).

• Move it to the pool. Open the Regular Pay envelope and tap → Move $… to pool (the button shows the amount; no “are you sure” — the toast that follows has Undo).

• Fund your envelopes. Tap Available to fund on Home (or More → Fund on a phone). The pool balance is at the top. Apply your funding plan, or split it by hand, and confirm when the footer says Fully assigned ✓.

Paid more than once a month? Save a plan per paycheck and give each its How often (Paycheck 1, Paycheck 2…). Home’s Funded card then shows Paycheck 1 ✓ · Paycheck 2 waiting.

That is it — every dollar of the paycheck has a job. Money left over goes to a savings envelope; never leave dollars jobless in the pool for long.

🧾 During the week — approve your spending

As you spend, transactions flow into the Inbox on their own (it syncs from your bank every hour; ↻ Check for new pulls from Tiller this second instead of waiting for the hour, and on the phone, pulling the page down does the same). A few times a week, clear it:

• Most items arrive with a suggestion pill — one tap to approve if it is right.

• Tick Always suggest this when approving and that store is suggested automatically from then on. The card shows the words the rule will look for (“Hannaford Supermarket”, not “Hannaford Supermarket 8123”); the message after has Edit rule. An Inbox card filled in by a rule says Suggested by rule … — tap edit to fix a wrong one right there.

• ✓ Approve all takes everything that already has a suggestion.

• One purchase, two jobs? Split spreads it across envelopes (the Walmart run that was half groceries, half a game).

• Tag as you approve: every Inbox card has a + tag opener, and the tags you add there ride along with the approval — one entry in History, one Undo.

Settings → Suggestion rules lists every rule in the order SBC checks them (the first match wins). Tap one to change its words or envelope, move it up or down, or delete it; the preview shows what it would catch before you save. A ⚠ means a rule above catches everything this one would.

Approving isn’t busywork — it is the moment you see your spending. That ten-second glance is what makes the budget real.

🚨 When an envelope runs dry

Eating Out is empty and it is only the 20th? That is the system working — it caught the overspend while you can still do something about it.

• Cover it: open an envelope with slack and use Move money. You are choosing the trade-off (“less clothes money this month”) instead of discovering it later.

• Or let it ride negative: negative envelopes are allowed — they stay red and roll forward until you cover them at the next funding. Nothing hides.

Moving money between envelopes is normal and healthy. The budget is a plan, not a punishment.

💳 Credit cards — and why CC Payment stays empty

Cards are the one thing that trips people up, and the rule is simpler than you’d guess: a card purchase is spending, and it counts the moment you swipe. When the charge arrives in your Inbox you approve it into Groceries or Gas like any other purchase, and that envelope goes down right then.

So don’t put money in CC Payment 💳. When you pay the card bill, money leaves checking and the card balance drops; both sides land in CC Payment and cancel out, which is why it sits at about $0. Funding it would count the same spending twice.

Under Available to fund on Home, Cash in accounts is what is in checking and savings and Cards owed (amber) is the part that belongs to the card companies. Amber, not red — an obligation, not a problem.

Tools you’ll grow into

🎯 Goals — saving for something

A goal is an envelope with a finish line. Open Goals, tap + New goal, say what for, how much and — only if you want — by when. Leave the date blank and it’s a someday goal: no deadline, no nagging, just a bar that fills as you fund it.

The Goals page shows saved / target, the percent, and for a dated goal $X/month to make it plus On track or Behind. Behind gives you a month’s grace, so it won’t flip red the day before payday. Goal envelopes wear 🎯 in the Envelopes list and the nearest three sit on Home. Reached ✓ means spend it, or set a new number.

Max is the opposite tool: open an everyday envelope, Set max, and if it ever sits above that you see “$120 over max” on the row and under Needs attention. Tap it and Move opens with the amount filled in — you choose where it goes. SBC only points; it never moves your money. Caps show on the same Goals page wearing 🧱.

🏦 Autopay — will the cash be in the right account?

Envelopes answer “is this money spoken for?” They say nothing about which account it is sitting in. You can be perfectly on budget and still bounce a card payment because the cash is in savings and the autopay hits checking.

That’s what More → Obligations is for. Once per card or loan: what it is, which account it is paid from, how the amount is decided, the closing and due days, and how many days ahead you want warning. Nothing to update month to month — it reads your own balance history. The account pickers list every account your balance history reports on; an account marked Hide on the Tiller Accounts tab never appears here.

• Statement balance — what the card owed just after it closed; what autopay-in-full actually takes.

• Whatever is owed today — for a card you pay off as you go.

• A fixed amount — a mortgage or car payment. You type the number.

The Obligations card on Home then lists what leaves in the next 30 days with the account it comes from. Green: the money is there. Amber: it isn’t, but you have it elsewhere — “Move $340 to checking before the 15th for Discover”. Red: not there and not anywhere else. Grey: that account’s balance hasn’t moved in days, so SBC won’t guess — see “When a balance is frozen”.

“I moved it” hides the warning for three days and records nothing. That is deliberate: the real transfer arrives in your Inbox on its own as two transactions. It is deliberately pessimistic — today’s money against what leaves, never counting on a paycheck first. Want the answer this second? Obligations → Check now.

🗓️ Budget & paychecks

Settings → Budget & paychecks decides what Home’s Funded card measures you against. Default is your funding plans: each plan has a How often (once a month, every paycheck, or paycheck 1 / 2 / 3), and each is In the budget Automatic (the plan you used most recently for that How often), Always or Never — so old or seasonal plans can stay saved without counting.

You can switch to Monthly targets (a fixed amount per envelope per month) or to no budget, in which case the card simply shows what you funded.

📈 Wealth — net worth, and can you retire?

Wealth (part of Reports - its five pages are the menu's Wealth group; on a phone, More → Reports & Wealth) answers one question first: can you retire when you want to? - then shows what your net worth can actually do: what is investable, what is a promise, what only sells on someone else's terms, and what you cannot spend.

Every number comes from your accounts: Tiller's Balance History (and, where it was imported once, history from before Tiller). Net worth here is always the same figure as Balances. Tabs today: Snapshot · Growth · Analysis · Planning · Estate.

Sections hide themselves when they have nothing to say - no debts, no debt table; nothing employer-tied, no exposure table. The Wealth settings gear on the page sets how each account counts (tier, tax type, employer-tied, yield, a loan's rate and payment, estate fields). It never changes Tiller or your budget.

On Home, the Wealth card shows net worth, its 12-month change and the verdict, and opens the page. It needs nothing extra from your phone - its numbers arrive with the rest of Home (hide it in Customize if you like).

Every label has an ⓘ - hover (or tap) for what it is, how it is figured and where it comes from. The same text, in full:

Earliest retirement age at which the plan funds your annual spending to the plan-to age in the base case; the pills show the other two cases. Source: Planning inputs + the projection engine (edge fn); re-run on every input change and nightly.

Net worth — Everything you own minus everything you owe. Sum of latest balance per account (liabilities negated), Hide honored. Identical to the Balances page by construction. Source: Tiller Balance History (mirror) + manual accounts; imported history before Tiller.

After-tax investable — What the withdrawal math is allowed to count. Investable + Deferred − (pre-tax balances × your tax-rate input). Illiquid is excluded on purpose. Source: Account tax type (Wealth settings) + tax-rate input on Planning.

Investable — Money that can fund retirement without selling something hard to sell. Sum of accounts tagged Cash, Taxable or Retirement (tiers 1–3). Source: Liquidity tier per account — Wealth settings; defaults from Tiller class/type.

Deferred · unsecured — Deferred compensation. Accounts tagged tier 4. Counted separately because it is an unsecured promise paid on an election schedule, not an asset you hold. Source: Liquidity tier per account — Wealth settings.

Illiquid — Assets you cannot turn into cash on demand. Sum of accounts tagged Illiquid: home equity, insurance cash value, 529s. Source: Liquidity tier per account — Wealth settings.

Semi-liquid · pays income — Sellable, but only on the buyer's terms (a company buyback, say). Accounts tagged tier 5 (semi-liquid). Semi-liquid — Sellable only on the buyer's terms. Tier 5 accounts. On someone else's terms and timing; may pay a dividend. Source: Tier 5 accounts.

Estate coverage — How much of the estate has a recorded plan. Balances of accounts with beneficiary / titling / trust status set ÷ net worth. Source: Estate tab per-account table.

By account — One row per account you have not hidden. Latest balance, share of net worth, liquidity tier, tax type, 12-month change, and flags. Source: Balance History + Wealth settings.

Account — Account name as it appears in Tiller. Display label can be overridden in Wealth settings. Source: Tiller Accounts tab.

Balance — Latest known balance. Most recent Balance History row for the account (date desc, time desc). Source: Tiller Balance History / manual balance.

🔔 Notifications

SBC Money can tap you on the shoulder instead of waiting for you to open it. Turn it on once per device in Settings → Notifications — on a computer in a normal Chrome or Edge tab, on an iPhone from the Home Screen app.

You choose which of four things to hear about: transactions to approve, a bank balance that stopped updating, a bill coming due that is short, and sync or save problems. Each is announced once, not every hour it stays true, and nothing is sent between 9 PM and 7 AM in your time zone (Settings → Personal information).

Tap a notification and the app opens on the page it is about; once the thing is handled, the notification clears itself on every device. The Inbox count also shows on the browser tab and the Home Screen icon.

📊 Checking on yourself

• Home (the Dashboard) — available to fund, Funded, Spent this month, Budget health, Obligations, Goals, recent transactions. Tap Customize at the top to show, hide or reorder the cards.

• Envelopes — every balance at a glance; negatives in red. Tap one for its full Activity (every movement names the other envelope — tap to jump). With dozens of envelopes, type in the Find an envelope… box at the top. Hide from lists quiets an envelope you never want to see (it stays pickable, leaves the list, and stops counting toward available to fund); Archive is for one that is finished for good.

• Transactions — everything approved; search by description, filter by account or envelope, un-approve or re-split, tap + tag on a row, or 🏷 Tag many to tag a whole trip at once.

• Reports — pick Weekly / Monthly / Quarterly / Yearly and step through periods with ◀ ▶; every report is scoped to your spending accounts and filters by group, envelope, or tag. Start on Period summary.

• Tags — label transactions with events that cut across envelopes (“2027 Myrtle Beach Vacation”). Tags also write to the Tags column of your Tiller sheet, so nothing lives only in SBC. Tag totals get their own report under Reports → Tags.

When something looks off

🩺 Is the budget honest?

Budget health on Home answers one question: does the money in your envelopes add up to the money you really have? It always shows the answer to the dollar — ✓ 100% accounted for, with every part of any difference named:

• Awaiting approval — transactions in your Inbox. Approve them and that part closes.

• Not imported yet — Tiller has them, the Inbox doesn’t. They arrive on the hour, or now with Check for new.

• In the balance, transaction not in yet (or the reverse) — normal bank timing; it settles by itself.

• Amber — an account out of step for more than a few days, usually a feed that stopped. Refresh it at my.tiller.com.

• Red, “not traced yet” — money SBC can’t tie to any account or transaction, with the day it started. Look at what changed that day.

🧊 When a balance is frozen

Once in a while a bank feed keeps reporting the same balance day after day while your real account moves on. Tiller writes a fresh row every night, so the date says “today” and everything looks healthy — the number underneath is just old.

SBC watches for it: an account whose balance hasn’t changed in days while transactions kept arriving gets a banner above Home and one email a day until it is sorted. Anything that depends on that balance says Can’t check rather than guessing — including your autopay warnings. SBC will never raise an alarm, or clear one, on a number it doesn’t trust.

To fix it: refresh or re-link that account at my.tiller.com. If the bank simply won’t cooperate, mark the account manual and set the balance yourself under Settings → Maintenance → Reconcile accounts.

Frozen is not the same as slow. On that page the Feed check lists two different things: an account whose balance moved by more than its transactions explain (interest, a fee, a slow purchase — it clears on its own), and an account marked 🧊 that hasn’t reported a balance for three days or more, which won’t clear by waiting.

✋ An account with no feed — HSA, rewards, a car

Some things you want on the books have no bank feed at all: an HSA, a credit-card rewards balance, a 401(k), the value of a car. SBC can track those — you just type the number in when it changes.

Create the account in Tiller, not in SBC. SBC has no add account button anywhere; the account list you see on Balances and Reconcile is built from what your Tiller sheet reports. Add it on Tiller’s Accounts tab — and give it a Group (Checking, Savings, Credit, Retirement …). ⚠ The group is the step people skip, and an account without one quietly drops out of the honesty check and shows up later as an unexplained balance nobody can find.

It reaches SBC on the next hourly sync — a Tiller-side change only ever arrives on the hour, so if it isn’t there yet, that’s why.

Then, in SBC: Settings → Maintenance → Reconcile accounts, find it under 🔄 AUTOMATIC and tap Mark manual. It moves up to ✋ MANUAL. From then on Update balance is how you keep it honest — that writes the new balance back into your Tiller sheet, so both sides stay in step.

⚠ A manual account is a balance, not a spending account. It will never have transactions and nothing you spend from it touches an envelope. That’s right for an HSA or a car. It is not the way to track cash in your wallet — for that, keep an envelope and let the money live in the pool.

📮 When a save doesn’t reach your sheet

When you approve, move or tag something, the app updates instantly and then writes the same change to your Google Sheet behind the scenes — usually within seconds. Once in a while Google doesn’t answer, and the write fails.

SBC retries on its own every time you open the app. If a save still can’t land, a red “save never reached your sheet” banner at the top of Home says exactly what didn’t save and why. Tap Retry to send it again right now, or Discard if you already redid it by hand.

Why it matters: the hourly sync always trusts the sheet. If an approval never got there, the sync puts the transaction back in your Inbox and the envelope goes back to what the sheet says — a paycheck you approved can look like $0 again. That isn’t lost money; it’s a save that needs a Retry.

A save and its “send to the sheet” ticket are written together — one can’t exist without the other — and each ticket is sent exactly once: if two parts of the app try to send the same one, the sheet recognises the duplicate and answers with the first result instead of moving money twice. If Retry says “already being sent”, another send is in progress; give it a minute.

Approve all used to take up to a minute and a half for a big batch; it saves the whole batch in a few seconds, however many rows you approve. If the banner ever says “Apps Script was busy”, an earlier save was still finishing — the app waits a couple of minutes and retries by itself.

🔄 Making the app re-read your sheet

Settings → Maintenance → ↻ Refresh app data from sheet re-reads envelopes, inbox, transactions and journal straight from your companion sheet, right now, and then tells you exactly what happened: Refreshed … ✓, or Skipped because one of your own saves hasn’t reached the sheet yet (fix the save from the Home banner first), or Google Sheets didn’t answer (try again in a minute). A ✓ means the numbers you see are the sheet’s numbers.

You rarely need it: the app catches up on its own within the hour. Reach for it after a repair made directly in the sheet. It cannot pull bank balances forward — that’s ↻ Refresh from Tiller on Balances.

↩️ Made a mistake? Nothing is permanent

Every action in SBC Money is recorded and reversible. One tap: when you approve, move, or fund, the green confirmation at the bottom of the screen carries an Undo button for a few seconds — tap it and the action is reversed on the spot. Missed it? Open Settings → History & undo and tap Undo on the operation; nothing expires there.

One tap back, too: your phone’s back gesture (or the browser’s Back button) steps back through SBC Money — envelope detail to Envelopes, a Settings page to Settings — instead of leaving the app.

You can also un-approve or re-split any transaction from the Transactions page. Relax and click things — you can’t break your money.

🔐 Who can see your money

Your bank data lives in your Tiller sheet and your companion sheet, in your own Google Drive. Two service accounts have access so the app works: the SBC app account (an editor — it imports your transactions hourly and writes your categorizations back) and a read-only account that feeds the fast view. Scott is on neither sheet, is not on your access list, and nothing in the app shows him your numbers.

The honest caveat: Scott runs the server the app uses, so like the operator of any app he could technically query its database. He doesn’t, and the app has no screen that would. If that is not enough, say so — the alternative is running your own copy.

Settings → Maintenance → Access is who can sign in to your budget. You are its admin; add a partner’s Google email there and they see exactly what you see. Remove anyone any time. You can leave whenever you like: Tiller keeps your complete history in your own sheet.

🗺️ Every page, one line each

The pages the rest of this guide doesn’t stop to explain. If a page loads with a red ⚠ and a Try again button, tap it — the load failed, nothing else did.

• Transactions — every approved transaction; search by description, filter by account, envelope or hidden, and tag from here.

• Fund — moves pool money into envelopes. Type the amount (or take the whole pool), apply a plan or split by hand, and the footer says Fully assigned ✓ when every dollar has a job. Plans live at the bottom of this page.

• Envelopes — balances by group; tap a group header to fold it, + New envelope to add one, and the search box to find one. Envelopes and Tiller categories are one list: creating, renaming, regrouping or archiving an envelope here does the same thing to its category in your Tiller sheet (archive = Hide From Reports), and the confirmation says Tiller ✓ when it landed. Hide is the exception — it only quiets the envelope in SBC.

• Inbox — bank transactions waiting for an envelope; the badge on the tab is the count, and ↻ Check for new pulls from Tiller this second instead of waiting for the hour.

• Accounts — what Tiller knows about each bank account, and where you hide the ones you’re done with.

• Settings → Maintenance — the once-in-a-while tools live behind one row so the everyday list stays short. Open it for the ones below.

• Tiller sync — where SBC and your Tiller sheet disagree (bank edits, deletions, drift, missing categories) over the last few weeks. Nothing changes until you tap a ✎ Write, ✎ Add or ✓ Accept button. Envelopes missing from Tiller Categories lists envelopes made before the two lists were joined — ✎ Add puts them on the Categories tab in one tap.

• Reconcile accounts — bank balance vs. what SBC thinks, per account, with the Feed check and the manual-balance ✎. New accounts are added in Tiller, never here — see “An account with no feed”.

• Remove a transaction — for a duplicate or an orphan Tiller no longer has; anything Tiller still has is protected. Logged and undoable from History.

• Access — who can sign in to your budget; add a partner’s email here.

• More (phone) — the pages the five tabs have no room for: Transactions, Fund, Goals, Obligations, Accounts, Reports, and Settings. On a computer these all live in the left sidebar instead.

• Sign-ins & activity — every sign-in and who changed what (Settings → Records).

• Health settings — which account groups count as cash and debt for the honesty check (Settings → Set up).

Display density, Monthly dress-up, Back up envelopes to Tiller and the Customize button on Home are all cosmetic or belt-and-suspenders — none of them moves money.

🎨 Monthly dress-up & birthdays

On a computer, the logo and sidebar dress for the season (on a phone it’s the little emoji in the Home greeting) — snow in January, fireworks in July, a string of lights in December. It’s decoration only: nothing about your money changes, and the numbers never move.

Tell SBC your birthday (Settings → Personal information) and your birthday month adds balloons and confetti, and on the day itself the app greets you with Happy Birthday. The full date is saved with your account — the year will also power future age-based reports.

Not your thing? The Monthly dress-up switch in Settings turns it all off on that device — each person decides for themselves.

⬆️ Keeping the app current

SBC Money is a web page you’ve added to your home screen, and it keeps a copy of itself on your device so it opens instantly, even on a bad connection. When an update ships, a teal Update available — tap to reload banner appears at the top of Home within an hour or so. Tap it; the app reloads on the new version and nothing you’ve done is lost.

Settings → About shows the version on each part of the app — Server, UI and Mirror. They should match; if they don’t, tap the banner (or reopen the app once) and they will. The Build line under them names the exact release this screen is running.

📅 Your routines, on one card

• Payday (2 min): Inbox → approve paycheck → Move to pool → Fund with your plan.

• Every few days (1 min): clear the Inbox; glance at an envelope before a big purchase.

• Monthly (10 min): review Reports, adjust your funding plan to real life, cover any negative envelopes, glance at Goals — are you on pace?

• Once per device (1 min): Settings → Notifications → Turn on, so the app tells you when something needs you.

Once, ever (5 min): enter your autopays under More → Obligations — every card and loan that pays itself. After that it warns you on its own.

Consistency beats perfection. A slightly-wrong budget you touch every payday beats a perfect one you abandon.
