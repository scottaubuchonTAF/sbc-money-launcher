SBC Money

Privacy

SBC Money is a private, family-run envelope-budgeting app. This page says, in plain words, what it reads, what it keeps, who can see it, and how to leave.

What SBC Money is

A budgeting app built and run by one person for family and friends. It is not a company, it has no customers, and it does not sell, share, advertise with, or monetize anything. It runs on top of Tiller, a separate service you subscribe to, which puts your bank transactions into a Google Sheet that you own.

What it reads, and from where

  • Your Google account's email address, when you sign in with Google. That is the only thing sign-in gives the app — no contacts, no mail, no Drive browsing.
  • Two spreadsheets you own in your Google Drive: your Tiller sheet (transactions, account balances) and your SBC companion sheet (your envelopes, your choices). You share them with the app's service accounts yourself, during setup, and you can unshare them at any time.
  • Your Tiller account, only if you connect it (optional: Settings → Account & privacy → Tiller connection): the list of your Tiller sheets, and the status of each bank connection in your budget's Tiller sheet. See “Connecting Tiller” below.

Your bank login never leaves Tiller. The sheets hold account names with the last four digits only — the app never sees, stores or shows full account numbers or passwords.

What it keeps

To open quickly, the app keeps a copy of the rows in those two sheets in a database it runs (hosted on Supabase, in the United States). That copy is refreshed from your sheets every hour and rebuilt each time; your sheets stay the source of truth. The app also keeps your settings, your approvals and the notes you add, and a record of each time the database is read outside the app (see below). If you connect Tiller it also keeps a locked Tiller sign-in key, the Tiller login's email (locked) and each of your banks' connection status, until you disconnect — the full list is under “What SBC Money keeps if you connect Tiller”.

Who can see your money

You, and the people you add. Your data is visible to the Google account you set the app up with and to anyone you add to your budget’s access list (Settings → Account & privacy → Add a person); unless you add someone, nobody else is on it. The app has no screen that shows one person another person’s numbers. If you connect Tiller, everyone on your budget’s access list sees each bank’s connection status; only the owner or an admin sees the Tiller login’s email.

Two service identities, so the app can work: an app account (editor on your two sheets — it imports your transactions hourly and writes your categorisations back) and a read-only account that feeds the fast view. They are not people.

The operator — the person who runs the server. Like any app's operator he could technically query the database. He does not, and the app has no screen that would. Reading the database leaves a mark: every statement anyone other than the app itself runs against it is recorded and shown inside your own account at Settings → Account & privacy → Who has read the database, together with whether that recording is switched on. It records statements, not whose rows came back. He also holds the app account's password, and opening your sheets in Google Drive as that account is not in that record — your sheet's own Activity panel in Google Sheets is the record there.

If you connect Tiller, the operator’s check scripts can also read the status codes, the times and the counts, and the Account IDs they are filed under — never the key, the Tiller email, who connected it, your sheet’s ID, or any account or bank name (see “What SBC Money keeps if you connect Tiller”).

Connecting Tiller (optional)

Connecting Tiller isn’t switched on for every budget yet. If you don’t see Tiller connection in Settings, none of this section applies to you.

If you tap Connect Tiller, you sign in at Tiller and Tiller hands SBC Money’s server a key to your Tiller account. Tiller offers only one kind of key, and there is no read-only option. The key opens your whole Tiller account: every Tiller sheet you have, every bank account linked to Tiller (even ones that are not in your budget), and Tiller’s tools for reading, changing and deleting transactions and categories.

SBC Money’s program uses the key for three things, and only these:

  1. to list your Tiller sheets, so it can check that the one your budget uses is really yours;
  2. to read each bank connection’s status — working, needs you to sign in again at Tiller, or when Tiller last reached the bank;
  3. when you or an admin of your budget presses Refresh my banks, to ask Tiller to check your banks now. That uses one of your daily Tiller refreshes, the same ones as the Refresh button on the Tiller website.

The key is kept on SBC Money’s server (hosted by Supabase), locked with a second key that exists only in the server’s settings. The server is run by one person, the operator of SBC Money. Like the operator of any app, he could change the program to use the key for more than these three things. Reading the stored key from the database by hand is recorded on your Access page; a change to the program is not.

To stop: Settings → Account & privacy → Tiller connection → Disconnect. SBC Money deletes the key at once. Delete my account deletes it too. Tiller does not publish a way for an app to cancel its key on Tiller’s side, so a copy someone had already taken could keep working until Tiller expires it. If your Tiller account settings list connected apps, remove SBC Money there as well.

What SBC Money keeps if you connect Tiller, and who can see it

Only if you press Connect Tiller. Kept on SBC Money’s server for your budget, and nothing else:

  • The sign-in key Tiller gives SBC Money, locked with a second key that exists only in the server’s settings.
  • The email address of the Tiller login you used, locked the same way (only if Tiller sends one). It is shown only to your budget’s owner or an admin, in the “Use this Tiller login?” question.
  • Who connected it: the email address of the owner or admin who pressed Connect.
  • Which of your Tiller sheets your budget uses, and how many other accounts your Tiller login has — a number, never their names.
  • For each bank account in your budget’s Tiller sheet, filed under Tiller’s Account ID: Tiller’s short status codes (working, needs a sign-in, the bank didn’t send data …), the kind of account, and when Tiller last reached the bank and next plans to. No bank or account names, no balances, no transactions.
  • When Refresh my banks was last pressed, what Tiller answered (as a short code), and when the daily limit comes back.
  • A dated record of connects, confirmations, sign-in key renewals (and a key that stopped working), status reads (how many accounts, how many failed) and refreshes (how many bank connections) — short codes and numbers only, never Tiller’s own words. A sign-in SBC Money refuses (not your budget’s Tiller sheet, or a Tiller login another budget uses) leaves no record on a first connect. When you connect again, a refused or failed sign-in is noted as a short code and a time.
  • While a sign-in is in progress: a scrambled form of its one-time code, a locked check value, and the email address of the person who pressed Connect. It is removed when the sign-in is used, or the next time your budget is opened after its 10 minutes.

Disconnect deletes all of it at once, the dated record included, and so does Delete my account. An unanswered “Use this Tiller login?” is deleted, with its key, the next time your budget is opened after its 30 minutes.

Who can see it. Everyone on your budget’s access list sees the status words in the app; only the owner or an admin sees the Tiller email, in the question above. The operator’s check scripts can read the status codes, the times and the counts, and the Account IDs they are filed under — never the key, the Tiller email, who connected it, your sheet’s ID, or any account or bank name. Each of those reads is listed on your Access page. Like everything else in the database, the locked key and the locked email could also be read by the operator by hand (recorded on your Access page) or from a backup (not recorded); they stay locked without the server’s second key, which the operator controls.

What it does not do

  • No advertising, no analytics trackers, no selling or sharing of data with anyone.
  • No access to your Google account beyond the email address and the two sheets you shared.
  • No cookies beyond what the sign-in needs and what your browser keeps to remember you.

Leaving

In the app, Settings → Account & privacy → Delete my account. It deletes every row of yours from the database, takes you off the app's lists, and removes the two service accounts from your sheets — in one go, after you type DELETE. If you connected Tiller, it also deletes the Tiller sign-in key and everything listed under “What SBC Money keeps if you connect Tiller”. Your sheets stay in your Google Drive, and Tiller keeps your complete history in your own spreadsheet; Tiller itself is a separate subscription you cancel with Tiller.
